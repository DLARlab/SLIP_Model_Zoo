WHY EARLIER SEARCHES KEPT STOPPING
The old ledger's status was not a mathematical completion certificate.
Budget yields, failed return-map derivatives and exhausted retry strategies
left frontiers obstructed or held without proving endpoints. Small adaptive
steps near the clearance boundary could therefore consume repeated grants.
Separate raw fragments and numerical overlaps also required owned edges,
unchanged-state comparisons and explicit graph reconciliation before a
connection could be asserted. Equal speed or nearby stored states were
insufficient. Old metadata/selector assumptions, caller paths, custody
parsers, large diagnostic payloads and export/reader failures added real
workflow delays; their failed outcomes have not been relabeled as success.

This round repaired the event engine and checked 53 canonical cases plus
six matched old/current replays. It then used square forced-phase root
equations F7 plus an owned pseudo-arclength constraint, followed by the
original native BL return problem, full-state closure and all nine gates.
The phase chart is an acquisition aid, never a replacement native orbit.
No source state was snapped/projected to manufacture admission. Local
boundary systems used F7 plus initial clearance, or F7 plus g and dg/dt,
with rank/stencil checks and independently admitted inward controls.
Root-Newton and scalar roots were used; no optimization method was used.

For the 26 accepted LOW Extension01 attempts retained by the native
SavedDataInspection02 audit, median phase time was 6.48 s
and median native-service time was 41.08 s. The recorded attempt median
before the final checkpoint was 59.60 s; final checkpoint, queue and window
overhead are excluded. Thus a five-second predictor/corrector expectation
does not describe the current fully admitted/persisted workflow. These
measurements are sample-specific, not a promise for every frontier or gait.
No controlled runtime comparison with the v2 implementation was performed.
