# MATLAB research runner and checkpoint contract

`RunResearchRound_v3` is the public entry point for the versioned next round. It bootstraps the v3 numerical folders and uses MATLAB for configuration, source conversion, correction, continuation, experiment control, task/phase checkpoints, protection verification, graph construction, figures and report export. No Python wrapper or subprocess is needed.

```matlab
addpath(fullfile(pwd,'SLIP_Quadruped_v3'));
report = RunResearchRound_v3('Profile','validation','Resume',true, ...
    'MinRepairRounds',3);
report = RunResearchRound_v3('Profile','full','Resume',true, ...
    'MinRepairRounds',3);
```

The actual executed outcome and exact unfinished queue are exported to `Research_v3/next_round/report_<profile>.json`, `task_queue_<profile>.json`, and `Docs_v3/Next_Round_<Profile>_Execution_v3.md`. These records distinguish registration/export from numerical execution. `ExecuteNumerics=false` prepares and audits the checkpoint and reports; it does not execute the research tasks.

`MaxTasks` and `TaskIds` select an execution segment. They do not replace the registered total budget, change acceptance tolerances or mark the omitted tasks complete. The full experiment registers 10,800 seconds of numerical task wall time and 300,000 counted nonlinear/return-map evaluations, with 240-second task slices and 12-point continuation batches. The original validation profile registers 1,800 seconds, 30,000 evaluations, 90-second slices and three-point batches. A separately preserved prospective journal `profile_revisions_validation.json` increases validation slices to 480 seconds after the measured 90-second slice cost failure; the original registration and true 1,800-second total remain unchanged. Integration calls and map evaluations are reported by the underlying engines; counted evaluations are recorded per task, while the wall budget is the common limiting account. A point cap or task slice is a checkpoint rather than a mathematical branch endpoint. Budget exhaustion leaves an incomplete result with the exact pending queue.

The prospective domain comes from every available 29-row variable in the preserved original branch copies. Mechanical energy is evaluated algebraically from the mapped source state and mode; that algebraic value does not certify the state is physically admissible. Initial horizontal speed is recorded separately from drift divided by primitive period. The new energy/speed/period bounds include the historical high-energy P1 critical neighborhoods. Critical snapshots are copied byte for byte, their exact nested variables are extracted with their own physical parameters, and duplicate complete numeric arrays retain provenance aliases. `source_inventory.mat`, `source_inventory.json` and `critical_source_supplement_registration.json` preserve these distinctions. The previous registration remains a historical record.

The initial next-round E floor1.000001 was found to exclude physically admissible baseline P2 spread-source states with E≈.821700261. `domain_revisions.json` records the prospective lower-bound repair; original registration and earlier observations retain their original meaning. The journal applies to future source/P2 experiments, while the analytic ordinary vertical PIP stage explicitly keeps E>1. Physical admission of a mapped initial state does not imply periodic closure or a successful autonomous return.

The fresh protection baseline records the actual starting commit and working changes, including the pre-existing root prompt. `RoundProtection_v3` checks SHA-256, missing files and newly added files outside v3. It neither uses the old cleanup exemptions nor resets protected files. All checkpoint writers resolve their output through `V3OutputPath_v3`; MATLAB v7 compression and a strict 100,000,000-byte artifact limit protect ordinary GitHub commits.

Each task has an execution state, a separate scientific status, an attempt count, dependencies, elapsed wall time, evaluation count, new-point count and evidence path. States distinguish `pending`, `running`, `accepted`, `partial_checkpoint`, `retryable_failure`, `unresolved_obstruction` and `external_interruption`. A completed numerical attempt can remain a scientific failure. A historical completed stage never suppresses the three queued PIP candidates. Interrupted running tasks become explicit interruption records on resume; their last task/phase checkpoint remains the restart state.

The solver gate is accepted only when all required focused cases pass and the recorded SHA-256 of each numerical engine still matches the checkout. The source drivers call the shared `PeriodicSolutionSolver_v3`, which independently replays accepted solutions at tighter integration settings. Raw mapped candidates are saved before replay. Physical retraction, feasible-height/source-energy predictors and autonomous-apex rephasing are separate saved transformations, never silently substituted for source conversion or nonlinear correction. The family-specific P1/P2 target lists remain distinct: front-spread F2 and hind-spread H2 are independently assessed from state/contact histories.

The three source-repair rounds use distinct methods. A evaluates source conversion and direct fixed-energy correction in a state-qualified paired restriction. B uses measured physical/section failures to select neighboring columns, explicit stance retraction, a feasible height corridor at fixed mode/angles, a source-energy horizontal kinetic predictor when possible, and a subsequent autonomous apex. C increases source-neighborhood density and releases paired restrictions into the full admissible physical coordinates. It measures feasibility of the pitch/angle derivative stencil and increases predictor clearance from1e-7 to1e-3 only when that probe shows invalid coordinates, preserving the before/after transformation evidence. All primary source tasks use one BL touchdown per return, including primitive double-flight candidates. Separately named BL2 hypotheses and measured -1-mode candidates undergo the same full closure and minimal-period gates. An unavailable feasible corridor is retained as a particular predictor obstruction; it is not a nonexistence argument for a gait family.

The P1/P2 drivers execute signed physical-sector corrections after their source batch: fore/hind separation from PK, front and hind left/right separation from bound, and the missing pair separation from each half-bound orientation. The amplitude equation is solved with the additional energy unknown inside the shared corrector. P1 targets one positive-duration primitive flight and P2 targets two, with F2 and H2 retained separately. A corrected orbit does not by itself establish a critical eigenvector, branch point or ancestry. Accepted target-model in-domain source and sector orbits automatically register both continuation directions in `accepted_seed_manifest_<profile>.json`; their exact seed artifacts and actual physical symmetry charts are preserved.

`ExtendRoundBranch_v3` reads the historical imported PK, restricted daughter or newly accepted source/sector checkpoints, independently validates the restart, reuses the accepted endpoint tangent, appends new arclength and removes the shared seam. The separately registered targeted derivative and Broyden cost repair applies to synchronized-pronk continuations. The executed two-batch resume audit permits prospectively larger full-profile steps .02, then adaptive batches up to .1; replay/chart/tangent failure still shrinks the step inside recovery. Original branch files remain unchanged. `ProcessPIPCandidateQueue_v3` independently checkpoints identification, refinement, critical subspace, transversality, frozen signed predictors, daughter correction/replay, daughter continuation and attachment assessment. Resume continues the unfinished phase.

The new typed graph is exported under `Research_v3/next_round/graph/`. Imported/recovered orbit records, continuation segments, historical restricted attachments and unresolved targets have different node/edge types. Equal gait labels, repeated provenance records, a unit multiplier or a finite solver run do not establish branch identity, full-space differentiability, certified bifurcation, connected target coverage or global completeness.
