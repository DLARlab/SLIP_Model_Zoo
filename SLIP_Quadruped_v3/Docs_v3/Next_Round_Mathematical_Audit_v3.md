## Energy pivot and corrector rank

Let u contain d independent section coordinates, let P be the declared translation-reduced marked return, and let H be its pulled-back energy. At a fixed point where P is differentiable and conservative,

`DH(u)*DP(u)=DH(u)`.

Thus energy gives a structural left unit eigenvector; it cannot be counted as independent symmetry-breaking evidence. Select pivot j with `H_j != 0`, and use d equations

`F(u,E)=[(P(u)−u)_{−j}; H(u)−E]`.

The omitted closure is justified locally: if the other d−1 coordinates close and energy is conserved, P(u) differs from u only in coordinate j. Local strict monotonicity of H in that coordinate implies the remaining component closes too. This is a local rank argument, not permission to ignore numerical energy error or the omitted component. Away from exact closure its error can be amplified by `1/|H_j|`; every accepted candidate must undergo a fresh full physical closure check. The existing energy family selects the largest initial gradient component and explicitly rejects loss of that pivot's regularity. [Energy family residual](../4_Solution_Management/EnergyFamilyResidual_v3.m).

For arclength correction, unknowns are `(u,E)` and the arclength equation is inside the nonlinear corrector: d+1 unknowns and d+1 equations. A signed daughter condition similarly adds one equation, such as `v'*(u−u_parent(E))=a` with a nonzero signed amplitude. A sphere is an equation in this augmented system, with a nonvanishing constraint gradient at the corrected point. A post-hoc distance filter after an unconstrained parent correction does not supply this contract. Nonperiodic admissible trial states must retain their nonzero objective; continuous periodicity is a final acceptance condition.

## Local derivatives and simultaneous events

For a transverse isolated autonomous guard g with normal n, the event time derivative is `D tau=−n'*D phi_minus/(n'*f_minus)`. The common-clock jump linearization is

`S=DR+(f_plus−DR*f_minus)*n'/(n'*f_minus)`.

This formula includes the event-time shift; a reset Jacobian alone does not. Terminal section timing contributes `Pi=I−f*n_section'/(n_section'*f)`, followed by the actual translation gauge and physical-coordinate extraction. A finite difference of a redetected return already includes timing and must not receive a second saltation/projection correction. These expressions require smooth extensions, transverse guards, and stable local event identities. [Primary saltation derivation, Kong et al., III-A–III-C](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.06862v3).

There is a model-specific reason for this favorable first-order result. At a regular zero-compression contact, adding/removing that leg leaves body force and torque unchanged. Its reset Jacobian minus identity and its timing correction have nonzero rows only in that leg's angle/rate block and nonzero columns only in the body coordinates and that leg's own block. Write its saltation as `S_i=I+A_i`. For distinct legs, `A_i*A_j=0`; another leg's instantaneous reset also leaves the inputs determining `S_i` unchanged. Thus the limiting instantaneous factors commute under these zero-compression, regular-geometry and transverse-guard hypotheses. This is stronger than reset commutation alone. A whole return still needs continuous feasible itinerary extensions, correct section transport, no event creation/loss, and stable marking. First-order agreement does not supply C² hypotheses.

Commuting resets alone do not imply differentiability: the audit includes a two-dimensional example with identity resets, before-field `(1,1)`, fields `(1,2)` and `(2,1)` after the first respective event, and final field `(1,1)`. Its two saltation products differ despite identical composed resets. Conversely, finite-difference itinerary changes do not by themselves prove a nonexistent derivative. Agreement must be checked in common coordinates, on feasible cones, with refinement and physical chart transport. Agreement of instantaneous factors is only a local ingredient; it does not certify a complete return derivative or C² regularity.

Event-selected vector-field theory yields piecewise differentiability under specified smooth-extension and transversality hypotheses. Its theorems are not automatically a theorem for resets between contact manifolds of different dimensions. An appropriate embedding/extension and guard-cone argument is needed before invoking them. [Primary definitions and flow theorem, Burden et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/1407.1775v3).
