# Shared periodic-solution correction contract

`[solution, report] = PeriodicSolutionSolver_v3(seed, options)` uses `RootSolver_v3`, the physical mode chart, and `EnergyFamilyResidual_v3`. An empty solution means acceptance was not established. `report.final_candidate` and the numerical exit remain available after failure. The solver does not prescribe legacy contact times.

## Model and relative periodicity

The continuous state is `z=(x,vx,y,vy,phi,dphi,alpha_BL,dalpha_BL,alpha_BR,dalpha_BR,alpha_FL,dalpha_FL,alpha_FR,dalpha_FR)`, with contact mode `q=(BL,BR,FL,FR)`. The ten parameters are `[k_l_b,k_l_f,k_s_b,k_s_f,l_l_b,l_l_f,rsla_b,rsla_f,j_pitch,l_com]`. Gravity and body mass are nondimensionalized to one. `ContinuousDynamics_v3`, `GuardFunctions_v3`, `ResetMap_v3`, and `ModeTransition_v3` are the implemented model specification; source conversion does not alter these laws.

For hip offset `s_i`, absolute leg angle `theta_i=phi+alpha_i`, hip height `h_i=y+s_i sin(phi)`, and stance length `L_i=h_i/cos(theta_i)`, stance force is `k_i(l_i-L_i)(-sin(theta_i),cos(theta_i))`. Body acceleration is the summed force minus gravity; pitch torque is `sum s_i k_i(l_i-L_i) cos(alpha_i)`. Swing angular acceleration is the existing infinitesimal-leg Lagrangian expression in `ContinuousDynamics_v3.swingLegAcceleration`, including torso acceleration and torsional restoring force. It is not replaced by a separately prescribed contact oscillator.

Each stance leg has reconstructed anchor `a_i=x+s_i cos(phi)+h_i tan(theta_i)` and constraint `d(a_i)/dt=0`. Its angular rate is dependent: `dalpha_i=-dphi-[vx-s_i sin(phi)dphi+(vy+s_i cos(phi)dphi)tan(theta_i)]/[h_i sec(theta_i)^2]`. The retraction enforces this expression rather than treating a constrained stance rate as an independent Newton variable. On a regular mode, the manifold dimension is `14-n_stance`; removal of horizontal translation and apex phase gives `d=12-n_stance`. Symmetry restrictions reduce this further through an explicit lift, with matched parameters and modes checked before the synchronized-pronk restriction is admitted.

The guard is `g_i=h_i-l_i cos(theta_i)`. A swing leg enables the downward crossing for touchdown; a stance leg enables the upward crossing for liftoff. The first eligible directed root owns the contact. Body state is continuous; the affected angular rate is projected to zero horizontal foot velocity and the mode flips. Coincident admissible contacts form one physical batch with deterministic leg ordering for internal reset rows and post-reset/right-continuous ownership. Zero-duration intermediate rows remain in the event log but are not physical contact phases. Commuting resets alone do not establish differentiability of the return.

Mechanical energy on the finite-inertia physical manifold is `H=0.5(vx^2+vy^2+j_pitch*dphi^2)+y+sum_stance 0.5*k_i*(l_i-L_i)^2`. Fixed anchors imply `dH/dt=0`; the infinitesimal swing-leg states do not add finite mechanical energy. At contact roots the entering/leaving spring compression is zero and body velocity is unchanged, so this same H is continuous under the reset. `QuadrupedEnergy_v3` exposes the gradient, flow identity residual, and constraint report. Infinite inertia requires a separately justified restriction; this service refuses a generic energy family there.

A relative periodic solution obeys `Phi_T(z0,q0)=T_Delta(z0,q0)`: every continuous component except x closes, the discrete mode closes, and the translated reconstructed stance anchors close because their formula changes by exactly Delta when x changes by Delta and the other coordinates close. The full physical event history is retained. No leg relabeling is permitted by the acceptance test. No independent physical memory is added; numerical guard arming is part of event detection and must reproduce the same first eligible event from the state and mode.

## Section selection before closure

The principal section uses `vy=0`, downward orientation, and the BL mark implemented by `BLMarkedApexReturnPolicy_v3`. The initial apex must be the last downward apex strictly before the next BL touchdown: a lookahead to the next apex must contain that BL touchdown strictly between the two apices. Return after the declared number of BL touchdown occurrences to an apex whose next BL touchdown has the same strict mark and whose right-continuous mode closes. The policy counts BL events, handles intervening apices, and selects the occurrence before inspecting continuous closure. A BL touchdown coincident with the apex is outside this strict-before chart; it is not silently phase relabeled. The report stores initial/return marks, separating margins, return multiplicity, and event signatures.

Saved event-cycle orbits retain their explicitly selected event-cycle policy when supplied as orbit seeds. New state structs default to the BL-marked policy. Changing from an event-cycle section to the marked section is an explicit seed/rephase operation in the research catalog, not implicit selection by smallest residual. Event-cycle occurrence greater than one requires an explicit policy object; the BL policy directly supports a positive integer `occurrence`.

## Equations and rank

Let u denote independent physical section coordinates and P their declared return. In the full family chart choose energy pivot j at which `abs(dH/du_j)>=EnergyGradientTolerance`. The fixed-energy residual is `F(u,E)=[(P(u)-u)_{i!=j}; H(u)-E]`. It has d equations for d unknowns. If the local return preserves H, both points lie in the same regular chart, the pivot remains nonzero on their connecting neighborhood, and the other d-1 closure coordinates vanish, the conservation equation locally determines the omitted coordinate. This is a local rank argument, not an unconditional guarantee from a small reduced norm. Every physical closure component, including the omitted coordinate and all reconstructed stance rates, is verified after correction. A pivot losing regularity produces an explicit chart-boundary failure; a new chart must be constructed instead of silently changing equations mid-correction.

`FamilyConstraint='arclength'`, `'amplitude'`, or `'fixed-observable'` adds E as an unknown and appends `normal'*(w-reference)-target=0`, where `w=(u,E)`. Thus d+1 unknowns have d+1 equations. Pseudo-arclength uses a declared tangent/normal and reference; signed switching uses a declared projection normal with a zero energy component and signed target. A constrained correction is not by itself evidence of daughter ancestry. Orthogonality or sphere formulations requiring additional equations must introduce corresponding independent unknowns/rank reductions; this service deliberately rejects an extra constraint on fixed-energy d-unknown systems. It does not unconstrained-correct then post-check a requested distance.

The existing GUI second-seed sphere uses `SphereFamilyResidual_v3`: eliminate energy as `E=H(u)`, retain the d-1 independent closure equations, and append `norm((u-u_origin)./scale)-radius=0`. This gives d equations for d unknowns, with the sphere inside the nonlinear corrector. `FixedParameterContinuation_v3.secondSeed` delegates this residual to the same public service, including fresh replay and primitive-period acceptance. Its original radius/distance/scale report fields remain available to the GUI; a post-hoc distance check does not replace the sphere equation.

`ActiveCoordinateIndices` selects independent **full-state indices** in the symmetry chart, including its energy pivot. Frozen coordinates retain the seed values. Matching independent closure equations plus energy are solved; all frozen physical closure components remain mandatory acceptance conditions. Such a coordinate restriction is a numerical formulation unless an invariant-subspace argument is separately established. `ResidualScale` divides the equations; `CoordinateScale` scales the nonlinear unknowns. Both physical and scaled residuals are reported.

## Evaluation, numerical exit, and acceptance

Finite, physically admissible nonperiodic trial states return their nonzero objective. Guard, integration, chart, section, and discrete-mode failures remain invalid objective evaluations. Continuous periodicity is not a prerequisite for Newton or derivative evaluation.

`RootSolver_v3` now preserves `primaryConverged`, `primaryMessage`, `primaryErrorIdentifier`, `primaryExitflag`, iterations, invalid trials, map counts, and finite-difference column diagnostics separately from `finalizationMessage` and `finalizationErrorIdentifier`. It constructs an orbit only after numerical convergence and its full-closure check. An unconverged solve therefore retains its primary reason rather than throwing a secondary `EnergyFamilyResidual_v3:FullClosure` exception as if that explained the numerical failure.

The shared service registers reduced acceptance at most `min(FullClosureTolerance,EnergyTolerance)/100` (default 1e-10), with a still tighter Newton function threshold. This prevents the old default 1e-7 initial-residual shortcut from accepting a candidate that fails the fixed 1e-8 full closure gate. A candidate failing full closure is explicitly unsuccessful; no threshold is loosened to admit it. `MaxWallSeconds` prevents new map evaluations after the correction budget expires and preserves the last candidate; the evaluation cap includes derivative samples. A single running ODE call can exceed the boundary before returning; the wall budget is cooperative between map evaluations. Topology-compatible Broyden updates reuse a finite Jacobian, with a fresh derivative every five accepted steps by default. A measured correction that exhausted 180 seconds after four accepted steps was retried with this reuse and a registered 240-second slice, reaching full acceptance in eleven accepted steps; the saved failed trial remains evidence.

The root cache is local to one immutable residual/map, parameter and mode. Context-dependent evaluations bypass it, including overridden derivative integration settings. Acceptance constructs a **new** simulator and map with independently tighter top-level RelTol, AbsTol, event value/time tolerances and maximum step. It performs two fresh returns and records their difference to check deterministic ownership. Neither is a cached objective.

`SolverOptions.ReuseJacobian=true` with `InitialJacobian` now uses a finite matrix of the correct equation/unknown dimensions on the first Newton iteration. Previously the refresh test at zero accepted iterations always rebuilt it immediately. Caller provenance must establish matching physical parameters, mode, chart, equations, energy/constraint and scaling; dimensional checks alone do not establish that compatibility. Later refreshes, including the default five accepted-update interval, non-Broyden operation and invalidation after changed raw topology, remain active. `report.solver.output.initialJacobianUsed` records actual use; an already closed initial state does not count as using a derivative.

Default acceptance requires full non-gauge closure <=1e-8, energy variation <=1e-8, physical stance constraints <=1e-8, constraint residual <=1e-8, discrete closure, model-owned compression/nonpenetration inequalities, and no multiple cover detected within the declared bound. The exact correction policy and its BL occurrence are preserved when constructing the new replay map; orbit names alone do not reconstruct policy configuration. Positive-duration flight counts are reported separately from section-return multiplicity. `PrimitiveCycleCheck_v3` compares full state/contact histories without leg permutations for integer covers through MaxCover=8 and exposes inconclusive cover witnesses. In addition, every proper event-located apex of the fresh return is checked for full state/mode recurrence; a close witness is independently replayed with its declared apex occurrence. A shorter closed return rejects a repeated cycle even if uniform interpolation misses the cover. This is numerical primitive-period evidence within the declared return chart and bound, not proof of global primitivity. Failure produces an empty accepted solution with its candidate, residuals, partial trace where available, primary/finalization/replay failures, chart, event signatures and effective settings.

## MATLAB interface

Minimal seed fields are `state` (14), `mode` (4), `parameter` (10), `return_policy` (policy object or supported name), `occurrence`, and `provenance`. A `HybridOrbit_v3` or converted struct using `initial_state`, `initial_mode`, and `physical_parameter` is accepted. The parameter vector remains ten dimensional in every returned orbit; `[physical_parameter;energy]` is internal to family correction.

Options include `Symmetry` (`none`, `left-right`, `pronk`), `Energy`, `FamilyConstraint`, `Constraint`, `ActiveCoordinateIndices`, `ResidualScale`, `CoordinateScale`, `Integration`, `ReplayIntegration`, `SolverOptions`, `Acceptance`, `MaxWallSeconds`, and return limits. Existing square continuation residuals delegate through `Residual`, `Coordinates`, and `AugmentedParameter`; they must expose `Map`, `fullState`, `evaluateWithInfo`, and `createOrbit`. The service derives fresh acceptance replay from the resulting ten-parameter candidate orbit.

The experimental `TwistedReturnFamilyResidual_v3` and `OpposedSpreadFamilyResidual_v3` use this existing delegation seam: actual BL1 twisted equations drive correction, but exposed `Map`, orbit finalization and independent shared acceptance use actual physical BL2 returns. The front-only experiment remains unaccepted; the separately registered exact paired restriction passed predictor admission and meaningful nonlinear correction. Their rank, symmetry/marking scope, source epochs, classification limitations and raw evidence are documented in `Twisted_Return_Corrector_v3.md` and `Opposed_Spread_Corrector_v3.md`.

Actual focused outputs and registered tolerances are stored in `Research_v3/next_round/solver/`. `RunPeriodicSolverValidation_v3` executes known PIP/PK replay, a meaningful nonlinear perturbation, fixed-energy and augmented corrections, a failed imported seed saved before replay, the old reduced/full gap, and a reproduction of the saved parent trial. It also runs `ValidateMarkedOccurrence_v3`: an intentional two-cycle ordinary PIP retains BL occurrence two during independent replay, closes to approximately 6.12e-11, and is rejected as a multiple cover. Its JSON/MAT summary, pinned to solver/engine source hashes, is an execution gate for the next campaign. `ExportPeriodicSolverAudit_v3` exports the actual numerical summaries directly in MATLAB. Versioned logs identify pre-repair or interrupted runs; only a completed current-engine summary can open the full campaign.
