## 3 Derive the mathematical framework and its actual theorem conditions

Write a research-quality LaTeX methods document and a readable technical explanation using Definitions, Assumptions, Propositions, Proofs, and Limitations. Tie each object to code and artifacts.

Define the hybrid system with modes, mode domains, flows, directed guards, reset maps, event eligibility, and simultaneous-event semantics. Specify pre/post-reset ownership and whether execution is single-valued. Use local semiflows where appropriate; do not assume globally invertible flows for noninvertible resets.

Define complete hybrid trajectories, including contact mode and any essential auxiliary state. For horizontal translation action \(\rho_\ell\), define a relative periodic solution by

\[
z(t+T)=\rho_\ell z(t),\qquad T>0,
\]

including the discrete mode, event sequence, boundary convention and auxiliary-state closure. Define \(\ell/T\) as mean speed. Distinguish this from initial horizontal velocity. PIP requires zero drift and the stated synchronous vertical conditions, not merely one sample with dx=0.

Define the minimal relative period after translation reduction. Keep leg-relabelled shorter returns as spatiotemporal symmetries unless explicitly using a further quotient. Distinguish a primitive hybrid orbit from a repeated traversal and from an identical geometric trace with different contact history.

Identify the actual system symmetry group at fixed p by checking flows, domains, guards, resets and event rules. Distinguish symmetries of one fixed model from mappings between different parameter vectors. Do not assume S4 symmetry or fore/hind interchange at arbitrary front/back parameters or COM offsets. Treat reversers separately and prove their compatibility if used.

On translation-reduced trajectories \(\bar z\), define spatiotemporal isotropy, for example

\[
H_{\bar z}=\{(g,\theta):g\bar z(t)=\bar z(t+\theta T)
\ \text{for all }t,\ \theta\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\}.
\]

Use subgroup inclusion up to conjugacy to express symmetry loss. Separate pointwise spatial isotropy, spatiotemporal isotropy and reversing symmetry. Equal contact timings alone are not a proof of full-state symmetry. Maximal symmetry need not select a unique PIP orbit family.

Derive at least these propositions, stating all assumptions and missing hypotheses:

1. A fixed regular event itinerary with appropriate smooth flows/resets, transverse events, finite execution, and a transverse selected section gives a locally defined return map of the regularity warranted by those assumptions. Derive event-time dependence by the implicit-function theorem and composition. At contact clusters, prove the required smoothness or replace the claim by the appropriate piecewise/directional result.
2. Primitive relative periodic orbits correspond locally to fixed points of the correctly reduced, marked return map, subject to the declared cycle domain. Prove both directions, including event/mode closure and translation reconstruction.
3. A valid system symmetry maps admissible solutions to solutions. Derive the induced map on a section, including phase transport when the marked section is not symmetry-invariant. Establish the equivariance or conjugacy actually used for bifurcation analysis.
4. State a valid local branching theorem for an independent periodic-orbit residual. For a centered parent family F(0,mu)=0, a simple branch theorem requires suitable differentiability, one-dimensional kernel and cokernel of D_u F, Fredholm index zero, and a nonzero transversality condition such as
\[
D_{\mu u}F(0,\mu_*)v\notin {\rm Range}\,D_uF(0,\mu_*).
\]
Verify the hypotheses on an open neighborhood in the independent residual coordinates that contains the proposed daughter directions. Smoothness only within the parent's synchrony submanifold does not justify a theorem for branches that leave it. At an event-order boundary, establish a sufficiently smooth extension consistent with the adjacent admissible itineraries, or use an applicable nonsmooth argument. A pitchfork classification additionally needs the appropriate symmetry and nondegenerate reduced coefficients. Derive these conditions rather than asserting that a +1 multiplier proves a pitchfork.
5. For multiple critical modes, perform symmetry decomposition and a justified Lyapunov-Schmidt or equivalent reduction. A one-dimensional theorem does not apply directly to a multidimensional critical eigenspace. State exactly what local existence is proved and what remains numerical evidence.

Do not claim local theorems imply global connectivity, unique maximal roots, exhaustive gait discovery, or dynamic stability. Address possible disconnected components, secondary bifurcations, higher primitive periods, missing symmetry types, and singular itinerary boundaries.

Use verified primary literature, with accurate theorem conditions and bibliography. Relevant starting points are listed at the end of this prompt.

## 7 Validate independent Floquet and bifurcation analysis

Retain and improve Stability_v3/FloquetAnalysis_v3.m as an independent callable API accepting a solution and explicit options. It must recompute/validate the orbit as needed and return a structured result containing:

- Model, parameter, section and primitive-cycle identity.
- Independent physical coordinates, constrained/removed directions, and chart basis.
- Return derivative or directional derivative collection.
- Multipliers and eigenspaces only where mathematically meaningful.
- Numerical convergence, uncertainty, guard-transversality and event-topology diagnostics.
- A regularity status and explicit reason when ordinary Floquet analysis is unsupported.

For isolated transverse autonomous events, derive the saltation formula

\[
S_e=DR_e+
\frac{(f^+-DR_e f^-)\,n_e^\top}{n_e^\top f^-},
\qquad n_e=\nabla g_e(x^-).
\]

Compose flow variational matrices, event sensitivity, terminal section-time correction, and the actual translation/physical-manifold coordinate maps in the correct order. Do not double-count event-time projection.

Implement or retain independently validated finite differences of the physical return map. Each perturbation must remain in the physical section chart; its event times must be redetected or implicitly resolved by the same fixed model. Test a range of perturbation sizes and tighter integration tolerances; compare matrix actions against trajectory perturbations, not only eigenvalue agreement.

If event times are retained as shooting unknowns, distinguish that augmented residual from physical Floquet coordinates. Where guard equations H(x,tau)=0 are regular, derive

\[
D_x\tau=-H_\tau^{-1}H_x,\qquad
D_xF_{\rm eff}=F_x-F_\tau H_\tau^{-1}H_x.
\]

Use the same lifting when translating a physical critical direction into augmented solver coordinates. Do not interpret eigenvalues of the combined shooting Jacobian as Floquet multipliers.

At PIP/PK/BD simultaneous contacts:

- Establish feasible event-order perturbation cones.
- Check whether the limiting derivatives coincide; deterministic event tie-breaking alone does not prove differentiability.
- Before invoking a Bouligand derivative, establish local continuity and the applicable piecewise-differentiability hypotheses, including consistent zeroth-order limits across feasible event sequences. If these hold and the selection derivatives differ, retain the cone-dependent derivative with event-order metadata. If event sequences yield incompatible limiting reset states or another loss of continuity, report that obstruction instead.
- Do not average incompatible order matrices or treat one restricted matrix as a complete Floquet result.
- Do not infer stability solely from the individual selection-matrix spectra; account for admissible cone transitions or use a justified common contraction condition.
- Even agreement of first derivatives does not establish the higher regularity needed for a smooth pitchfork theorem.
- Use an applicable nonsmooth argument or report a nonsmooth candidate. Do not apply smooth theorems across grazing without justification.

Separate phase, translation, conserved-quantity/family neutrality, and additional critical directions by derivation. Do not remove a chosen number of eigenvalues nearest one. On an energy-preserving map, account for the structural unit left eigenvector associated with DE when DE is nonzero.

Detect and track critical invariant subspaces using spectral matching and rank/singular-value diagnostics; do not rely only on sorted eigenvalues or a sign crossing of a single multiplier. Refine critical parent orbits with uncertainty smaller than the claimed signal. Track folds, additional +1 modes, repeated modes, and relevant -1 or complex unit-circle events as distinct candidates.

For primitive-period checks, remember that squaring a map turns a -1 multiplier into +1. A repeated orbit is not evidence of an ordinary +1 symmetry-breaking branch.

At a candidate connection:

1. Refine the parent and the critical subspace in consistent coordinates.
2. Project away only justified structural directions.
3. Resolve symmetry sectors, including front- and hind-spread sectors.
4. Form signed small-amplitude predictors and use amplitude/orthogonality constraints to prevent collapse to the parent.
5. Correct daughters with full physical periodicity, admissibility and topology checks.
6. Obtain an amplitude sequence approaching the parent; verify critical-subspace alignment, full-trajectory distinction, symmetry change, and convergence.
7. Continue corrected daughters in both relevant directions and verify the attachment.
8. Freeze parent-only predictions before comparing held-out known daughter data.

Report each connection as theorem-backed under verified assumptions, numerically supported, or unresolved. A +1 crossing, an eigenvector plot, or one Newton-converged sample alone is not an accepted branch connection.
